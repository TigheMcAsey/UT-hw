    \section{Introduction}
    Ledrappier's 1999 paper \emph{Distribution des orbites des réseaux sur le plan réel} \cmt{ref} proves an ergodic theorem for averages of \(\slr\) orbits of real-valued functions. The goals of this document are twofold \emph{(i)} rewrite Ledrappier's paper in the language of Teichm\"uller theory, to better motivate and understand the result, and \emph{(ii)} to explicate terse elements of Ledrappier's proof.

    The following notation will be used throughout
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \(\mathbb{T}^2\) will denote the (topological) torus
        \item \(\mathbf{T_1}\) will denote the Teichm\"uller space of \(\mathbb{T}^2\) 
        \item \(\Gamma\) will denote \(\slz\), the mapping class group of \(\mathbb{T}^2\)
        \item \(\uqd\) will denote the space of unit quadratic differentials on \(\mathbb{T}^2\)
        \item \(\mathcal{M}\) will denote the moduli space \(\mathbf{T_1}/\Gamma\)
        \item Fix once and for all curves \(\alpha, \beta\) with homology classes giving a basis for \(\mathbb{T}^2\) and \(i(\alpha,\beta) = 1\).
    \end{itemize}

    \section{Teichm\"uller Theory for the Torus}
    \begin{defn}
        The Teichm\"uller space of the torus \(\mathbf{T_1}\) will be defined as the set of pairs \((S,\vp)\) where \(S\) is a Riemann surface, and \(\varphi: \mathbb{T}^2 \to S\) is an orientation preserving diffeomorphism, two pairs \((S_1,\vp_1),(S_2,\vp_2)\) will be identified if there is a biholomorphism \(I: S_1 \to S_2\), such that \(I\vp_1\) is homotopic to \(\vp_2\).
    \end{defn}
    In order to work with Teichmuller space, I will identify it with the space of abelian differentials of the form \(dx + \tau dy\) for \(\tau \in \mathbb{H}^2\). An abelian differential on \(S\) is a section of the holomorphic cotangent bundle \(K_S\), on the torus the transition functions are given by translation, \(z_\alpha = z_\beta + c\), thus the transition functions \(\frac{dz_\alpha}{dz_\beta}\) of \(K_S\) are constant, since the torus is compact any such section must be given by a constant function by the maximum principle. Now fix an abelian differential \(\omega\), we consider the period coordinates \((A,B) = \left(\int_{\varphi(\alpha)}\omega,\int_{\varphi(\beta)}\omega\right)\), the choice of \(\omega\) is only defined up to scaling, but we may normalize by replacing \(\omega\) with \(A^{-1}\omega\) to get period coordinates \(\left(1,\int_{\varphi(\beta)}\omega\right)\). Now considering the covering \(\mathbb{R}^2 \to S\), we get that up to homotopy \(\varphi(\alpha)\) corresponds to the deck transformation \((x,y) \mapsto (x+1,y)\), and similarly \(\varphi(\beta)\) to \((x,y) \mapsto (x,y+1)\), thus we get a unique representative \(\omega = dx + \tau dy\), for \(\tau = A^{-1}B\).
